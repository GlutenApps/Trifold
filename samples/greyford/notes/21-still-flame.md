A pale, steady fire, about the size of a fist, that needs no fuel and gives no heat. It is cold to the touch: holding the bowl it burns in hurts like gripping ice.

**Where it came from.** The founders of the shrine brought it from somewhere else; their statues sit in the keepers' hall. The shrine's oldest records were lost in a flood long ago. Hesper believes the flame was meant to guard the gorge from the start, and that the keepers were buried under their own rooms on purpose so they could go on watching.

**What it does.**

- **High and lit** on the gate in the middle of the gorge, it keeps the river in its bed and the dead of the shrine asleep, as far as its light reaches.
- **Off its gate,** it does the opposite: the river climbs toward it, and the dead wake and come looking for it. The shadows it casts become hungry things of their own.
- It **speaks in borrowed voices** to anyone who wants something badly enough. Corvin hears his sister. It isn't her.

**Relighting.** Once moved, the flame has to be set back in its housing on the gate with a short spoken form. Lantern-keepers learn the words from each other. Hesper is the only one living who knows them, unless she teaches someone.

**Hooks for later.** Who made it? Are there others, on other rivers? Corvin's old order would very much like to know where he learned that it could be moved.
