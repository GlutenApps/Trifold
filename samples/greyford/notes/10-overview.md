**For four level-3 characters. One session, three to four hours.** At the end the party reaches level 4.

## The situation

Greyford is a village on the old east road. An hour's walk north-east, the river Grey runs through a deep gorge, and in the gorge stands the Shrine of the Still Flame: a cluster of old halls, bridges and wind-wheels built on the rocks above the water. For two hundred years a lantern has hung from the great gate on the rock in the middle of the gorge. It burns with the _still flame_, a pale fire that never flickers and never needs oil. While it hangs there, the river stays in its bed and the dead buried in the shrine stay asleep.

Three nights ago the lantern went dark.

## What's really going on

**Brother Corvin Ashe**, a priest thrown out of his order and once the Lantern-keeper's student, has taken the still flame down from the gate and into the shrine hall. Ten years ago, the one other time the lantern went out, the Grey flooded and took his sister Lissa. He believes the flame, fed in the shrine, will call her back out of the river. It won't. Off its gate, the flame wakes the dead and draws the river up after it. The longer it burns below, the higher the water climbs.

Corvin needed time and privacy, so he paid **Mara Venn's** bandits in old shrine silver to hold the bridges at the mouth of the gorge. Mara's enforcer, **Gorm Tallow**, has been squeezing Greyford for "road money" on the side.

**Hesper Quill**, the Lantern-keeper, is tied up in the shrine's storehouse, along with **Tobin Marsh**, the sawyer's son, who went up the gorge to see why the light was out.

Both gorge fights happen on the same map. Its **Flood** background is the river at its worst: switch to it during the last fight.

## The clock

Each hour the flame burns below, the river rises and more dead stir. If the party dawdles (a long rest in the village, a detour), add two skeletons to the keepers' rooms and one more shadow to the last fight. Tell them so in the fiction: the shrine's bell rings by itself, once, at each hour, and the sound carries all the way to Greyford.
