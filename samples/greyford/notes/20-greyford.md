A village of about two hundred people at a crossroads on the old east road. It makes its living from timber, from the road, and from travellers who stop at the inn because there's nowhere else for a day in either direction. The river Grey runs through its gorge an hour's walk to the north-east.

## Places (on the village map)

- **The Wayside Lamp.** The big inn just south of the crossroads. Oswin Barrow runs it. There's a good fire, bad ale, and excellent mushroom pie.
- **The timber yard.** North of the road, stacks of planks around a sawhouse. Pell Marsh is the sawyer.
- **The toll house.** The little stone house on the road at the east end of the village. Nobody has collected a toll there in fifty years, until Gorm started.
- **The reeve's house.** The walled house with the pond and the fountain at the west end. Reeve Agatha Thorne keeps order, such as it is. She's the one who sent to the bandits asking for terms, and she'd prefer nobody mentions it.
- **The oak green.** A ring of lane around one enormous oak, south of the road. Every wedding and every argument in Greyford happens here.
- **The standing stones.** North-west, in the trees: a ring of old stones where travellers pitch their tents.
- **Widow Tansy's house.** One of the cottages by the oak green. Corvin lodged here a fortnight ago and paid in old silver stamped with a lantern. He left a stack of letters in the grate, all burned but one corner: _…Lissa, come home…_

## Things everyone in Greyford knows

- The lantern has never gone out in living memory, except once, ten years ago, for a single night. That was the night the Grey flooded and took Lissa Ashe.
- The Lantern-keeper always comes down on market day. She didn't come this week.
- Nobody goes into the shrine's kitchen or library after dark. Nobody can say why. It's just not done.
